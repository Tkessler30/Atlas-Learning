# Atlas Teaching Content Standard

A screen is not a lesson merely because it has a heading called “Learn.” Atlas considers a learning object instructional only when it gives the learner usable subject matter.

## Minimum contract for a normal lesson

- **Objective:** what the learner should be able to explain or do afterward.
- **Key terms:** concise definitions needed for the lesson.
- **Explanation:** concrete facts + causal/mechanical reasoning; no meta-prompts standing in for content.
- **Worked example:** a specific scenario or calculation carried through step by step.
- **Connection:** only when meaningful; names the shared mechanism and its limits.
- **Common mistakes:** actual misconceptions, not generic “be careful” warnings.
- **Takeaway:** one precise mental model.
- **Guided practice:** applies what was just taught.
- **Check:** content-specific and answerable from the lesson, with “I don’t know yet.”
- **Feedback:** explains why an answer is correct/incorrect.

## What is explicitly rejected

These are prompts, not teaching:

- “Focus on the mechanism behind X.”
- “Use a concrete case.”
- “Compare X with something you already know.”
- “Explain X in your own words.” (before X was taught)
- A generic quiz that rewards “explain the mechanism” regardless of subject.

## Evidence rules

- Placement shows prior knowledge; it does **not** prove Atlas taught the learner anything.
- Retrieval is scheduled only after a prior Atlas lesson.
- Open-resource background without a validated check is exposure evidence only.
- A fallback/unavailable response cannot produce verified mastery.
- A cross-domain connection cannot be verified from an instruction-only reading.
